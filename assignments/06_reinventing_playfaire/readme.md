Responding to the word 'boundaries', or using your own capstone/thesis project: find data, envision a way to investigate and understand it visually, and build a set of visual analytic products to reveal and extract something from it.

Beyond plotting.  As William Playfair, John Snow, Florence Nightingale, and others famously invented new ways to 'draw' relationships through data, today we face increasingly novel and complex problems for which we need novel approaches.  

Invent.  Reveal.  Extract.  Visualize ideas, relationships, and tensions.   Draw these to the screen and page.  Do more than show your data.  Show why we care.

Thinking with images.

The entire focus of the semester has been on using principles of visual design to generate contexts and meaningful readings of difficult or complex information. Data Visualization is not just a way to render data in a 'prettier' way, but is a structure to organize, understand, and reveal complexity.

Aesthetic Integrity... is not a measure of how beautiful an application is. It's a measure of how well the appearance of the app integrates with its function."
-- Macintosh Human Interface Guidelines, 1984.
Visualizing is also a process of iteration; a process of design.  Be intentional.
Ask a question, gain insight, and then ask a better question! The best visualizations are tools.

Take a position on your data.
Make an argument!
Pull it apart. Layer it. Challenge it.  Annotate it.  Expose what is meaningful about it.

What matters most is how you are going about this - your question, your methods, and your process for getting to deeper insights through visualizing – and your best efforts to make it all happen.  Yes we want to produce an amazing, rich, and effective final product, and technology is a part of this process, but this is not about the technology. In the end, this is about thinking through visual/spatial structure.

Deliverables:
an Interactive, Web-enabled, Data-Driven Visualization: using Observable/D3 or Tableau. This visual will be shown on your laptop screen, and can be manipulated by a viewer to highlight, filter, hide and reveal, re-orient data, or whatever you need. Use dynamic capabilities as a purposeful and appropriate component of data and design.

More than a graphic, this is a tool for organizing and exposing relationships.  It is a tool for problem solving.

Use Observable/D3 for its robust, flexible, and precise control capabilities, or use Tableau (published to Tableau PublicLinks to an external site. or our Tableau online siteLinks to an external site.) as may be appropriate.  This is not about the technology.  Your investigation, your questions, and your output as an insightful visualization product should be the same for any platform you choose.

a Static Visualization: to extract and expose your questions and insights within a single, static, visual field.  Show what is important, why we should care, and tell the story on a single page.  This is not a separate invention - it is just another version or lens upon your dynamic visual, and couples directly with it.  It extracts your reading.  So What?  Expose that.  This is not a screen-shot.  Extract (svg) and then clean up, annotate, focus, layer, highlight, reveal.  Don't assume we can just see what you're thinking -- show us and tell us your argument.

Written Explanation and Reflection: like usual, explain your work, why you did it, what you were after, why specific visual strategies, and what the sources are behind it.  Reflect on your process and approaches, challenges, techniques, and anything else that you want to include. What did you find, and how do these visuals get you there?  Sketches and screenshots from work along the way should be a part of this too.  Talk meaningfully about this project and what you're doing with it.  
All work should be submit to the Canvas site under this Final Project Assignment by the due date, and you should be able to fully show your work on your own laptop for the final showcase event.  The static visual should be printed or on a separate screen to show along side your dynamic.

Have fun, ask questions, we're willing to help.
Aspire to the best of what we have been looking at all semester long.
