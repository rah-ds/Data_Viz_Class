Ten Lenses on
Wikipedia Conflict
"Edit wars about facts outlast edit wars about people."
Ten visualizations that trace how — and why.
01
Who Stays to Fight?
For each of the 53 cases, the fraction of editors still active at each point in the normalized timeline. Thin lines are individual cases; bold lines are group averages. Move your cursor across the chart to read off exact retention at any point.
The departure paradox. Religion and Social cases lose half their editors before the halfway mark — yet those cases run just as long as Science cases where editors persist. Departure doesn't resolve conflicts. It just changes who's fighting.
02
Where Does the Time Go?
Average share of total events per editing phase, broken out by group. Larger circle = more of the case's activity happened in that phase. Hover any cell to see exact figures.
Evidence is the bottleneck, not the ruling. Science and Geopolitics cases spend over a third of all events in the evidence phase — far more than any other group. Once the committee has gathered enough, it acts fast. What takes time is gathering it.
03
How Do Cases End?
Each dot is one case. X-axis: share of total events in the final 20% of the timeline — high means the case was still active near the end. Y-axis: total duration (log scale). Dot size: number of editors. Hover any dot to identify it.
Two completely different resolution mechanisms. Politics cases cluster bottom-left: short, front-loaded, settled by a clear ruling. Geopolitics and Science cases drift top-right: long, with sustained activity right up to the end. They're not resolving — they're exhausting.
04
The Pulse of Conflict
Average fraction of events per normalized time window, by group. Each line is a group's "heartbeat." A sharp early spike means front-loaded drama; a flat sustained line means ongoing grinding conflict.
Politics burns fast; Science never stops. Politics cases front-load almost all activity — a sharp spike in the first 20%, then quiet. Science and Geopolitics show flat, sustained edit pressure all the way through. Fact wars don't peak. They grind.
05
How the Story Arc Changes
Each panel shows one group's average phase breakdown across the case timeline (20 equal windows). The colored stack reveals which editing phase dominated each window, showing how the narrative structure differs by category.
Evidence is a trap Science can't escape. In the Science panel, the orange evidence band stays wide well past the halfway mark. Politics clears evidence quickly and moves to a decision. Once framed as a factual dispute, the process locks into gather-more mode indefinitely.
06
Bigger ≠ Faster
Each dot is one case. X-axis: unique editors (log scale). Y-axis: duration in days (log scale). Dot size scales with total edit volume. If more editors meant faster resolution, you'd see a downward slope.
More voices, more noise — not more resolution. Across all groups, adding editors does not shorten cases. Science and Geopolitics cases with the most editors also run the longest — coordination overhead compounds the problem.
07
The Drop-Off
Each dot is one case, placed by the share of editors who made their last edit in the first half of the timeline. High x = editors left early. Vertical tick marks show each group's median.
Departure doesn't defuse — it hands the keyboard to the committed. Religion and Social cases shed the most editors in the first half, yet don't resolve faster. What remains is a smaller, more entrenched group. Science retains editors longest — the debate never feels "won."
08
53 Conflicts, 60 Moments Each
Edit intensity across all 53 cases (rows) × 60 normalized time windows (columns). Brighter yellow = more edits per day in that window. Cases are grouped and sorted by duration within each group.
Fact wars smolder; people wars explode. Politics rows show a bright spike at the left then darkness. Geopolitics and Science rows show a long, medium-bright band across the full width — consistent, sustained intensity. Policy rows are the darkest: low-drama procedural work that never catches fire.
09
How Long Is Too Long?
Each dot is one case, plotted by duration on a log scale. Rows are groups. Vertical tick marks show each group's median. Spread reveals which arenas are most unpredictable.
Geopolitics is the wild card. It has the widest duration spread of any group — some cases wrap in weeks, others drag for years. This reflects the cyclical nature of geopolitical flashpoints: a new real-world event can reignite a "closed" dispute at any time.
10
Beginning, Middle, End
For each group, the average phase breakdown in three acts: early (first 20%), middle (next 60%), and late (final 20%). Each horizontal bar is 100% wide and colored by phase. Shows how narrative structure differs by category.
Politics resolves in Act I; Science is still arguing in Act III. In political cases, the article and outcome phases wrap up by act 3. In Science and Geopolitics, the evidence phase stays prominent through the final act — the argument never settles because facts remain disputed.
