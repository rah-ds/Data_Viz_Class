The Revert Web
Who undid whom — the social graph of reversal

A revert is not just a technical undo — it's a declaration of conflict. When editor A reverts editor B's work, they are saying: your contribution does not belong here. This force-directed graph maps every revert in a single arbitration case as a directed edge between editors. Nodes sized by total edits; edges weighted by revert count. Cases with the most reverts tend to show a cluster-and-periphery pattern: a few combatants at the center, surrounded by bystanders who tried to intervene. 02
The Edit Pulse
When do editors fight? Hour of day × stage of conflict

Wikipedia runs on UTC and its editors span every timezone, but conflict isn't evenly distributed across the clock. This heatmap maps every edit event in every case to two dimensions: its UTC hour (rows, 0–23) and its normalized position in the case lifecycle (columns, 0–100%). Darker cells mean more editing activity at that hour during that stage. The emerging pattern reveals not just when Wikipedia is busiest, but whether early conflict (left) and late resolution (right) happen at different times of day — and whether late-night editing is correlated with the most contentious moments.

03
Case Metabolism
Fast & explosive vs slow & smoldering — mapping case tempo

Not all arbitration cases are equal in pace. Some erupt quickly — dozens of reverts compressed into days — while others drag on for years as editors grind through procedure. This scatter plots each case by its duration (x-axis, log scale) against its "conflict density" — reverts per 1,000 edits (y-axis). Bubble size encodes the number of editors involved; color encodes the primary topic. Top-left cases are the flash-fires: short, hot, and turbulent. Bottom-right are the long sieges: months of slow attrition. Hover to read the case name and key stats.

04
The Departure Wave
Role by role, who leaves — and when

Every arbitration case starts with a crowd and ends with a committee. Participants file their evidence then vanish; observers comment and drift away; arbitrators alone persist through to the final decision. This stacked area chart tracks the fraction of editors in each role who remain active at each normalized stage of the case, averaged across all 101 cases. The shape reveals the social physics of arbitration: the early surge of participation, the thinning of the middle game, and the quiet of the verdict phase where only the arbitrators are left speaking.

05
The Edit Score
Each case as a musical score — reading the rhythm of conflict

A musical score shows when instruments enter, when they play loudly, and when they fall silent. This view renders the 20 most edit-dense arbitration cases as stacked bar "scores" — each row is a case, each column is a 5%-wide slice of normalized case time, and bar height encodes edit volume. Reverts are marked in red; sanctions in gold. Reading horizontally, you hear the rhythm of each case: a steady hum, a sudden burst, a long silence, then a flurry near the end as arbitrators finalize. Cases are sorted top-to-bottom by their peak conflict intensity — the loudest cases at the top.
