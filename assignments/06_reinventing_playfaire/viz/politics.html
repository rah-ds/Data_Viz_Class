01 / When They Fought

The 20-Year Arc of Political Conflict

Each bar spans the duration of one arbitration case on the Wikipedia calendar. Width encodes duration; height encodes total editors. Gaps between cases are real — years where no political arbitration was filed.

02 / Phase Portraits

How Each Case Divided Its Time

Each ring shows the proportion of edits made in each procedural phase. Cases dominated by evidence-gathering have a different flavor than those dominated by proposed decisions.

03 / Escalation Curves

How Quickly Did Each Case Accumulate?

Cumulative edits plotted against normalized case time (0–100%). A line that rises steeply early signals a fast-burning conflict; a steady diagonal suggests sustained engagement throughout.

04 / Who Left Early

Entry vs. Exit — Mapping Editor Departures

Each dot is one editor. The diagonal represents an editor who stayed the full case. Dots below the diagonal left before the case closed. The further below, the earlier they departed.

05 / Role DNA

The Disappearing Arbitrators

Role composition per case. Over twenty years, the formal arbitrator class shrank and then vanished — later cases consist almost entirely of participants and observers, suggesting a structural shift in how Wikipedia adjudicates political conflict.

06 / Weekly Rhythm

What Day Do Wikipedia Editors Fight?

Edit intensity by day of week for each case. Brighter = more edits on that day. Patterns reveal whether disputes follow weekday work rhythms or spill into weekends.

07 / Editor Lifespans

How Long Editors Actually Engaged

Each dot represents one editor, placed at their fraction of the total case duration they were active for. Cases with dots clustered near zero had high churn; clusters near one indicate committed engagement.

08 / Evidence vs. Workshop

Two Modes of Arbitration

Cases lean either into evidence-gathering (building the factual record) or workshopping (negotiating remedies). This scatter maps each case's balance — with bubble size showing total edit volume.

09 / The Exit Pattern

Departure as the Unwritten Sanction

Zero formal sanctions were issued across all nine politics cases. Yet in every case, editors left before resolution. This slope chart traces the journey from enrolled editors to those who stayed to the end — departure may be the only consequence that stuck.

10 / Conflict Fingerprints

Each Case's Unique Signature

Each row is a case divided into 60 equal time buckets. Each cell is colored by the dominant editing phase in that window — creating a visual "barcode" fingerprint of how conflict unfolded. No two cases look the same.

Data sourced from Wikipedia Arbitration Requests  ·  SARC 5400 Final Project  ·  University of Virginia
