11 / Parallel Coordinates

Seven Dimensions of Conflict

Each line traces one case across seven axes — duration, editors, events, departure rate, evidence share, workshop share, and reverts. Cases that cross paths are structurally similar; cases that don't are anomalies.

12 / Ridge Plot

The Shape of Each Conflict

Edit density over normalized case time (0–100%), estimated by kernel density. Cases with sharp peaks had concentrated bursts; broad ridges indicate sustained engagement. Each ridge is one case, stacked chronologically.

13 / Cross-Case Chord Diagram

The Editors Who Keep Showing Up

Arcs represent cases; chords connect cases that share editors. The thicker the chord, the more editors appeared in both cases. British Politics BLP and Canadian Politics share nine editors — the densest overlap in this data.

14 / 24-Hour Activity Rings

What Hour Do Wikipedia Editors Fight?

Each ring divides a day into 24 hours (UTC). Bar length = number of edits in that hour. The brightest spike is the peak editing hour. Politics, it turns out, happens at all hours — and different politics happens at different times.

15 / Absolute Streamgraph

Political Controversy Across Twenty Years

Each colored stream is one case, placed at its actual date on a 2004–2024 calendar. Stream height encodes daily edit volume. The long silences between cases are real — years passed between major political arbitrations.

16 / Departure Wheels

When Did Editors Leave?

Each spoke is one editor. The angle shows when they departed (12 o'clock = start, clockwise = time passing). Spoke length = how many edits they made before leaving. Editors at the outer rim fought longer and harder before walking away.

17 / Bubble Pack

Where Did the Editing Energy Go?

Cases as outer circles, phases as inner bubbles. Size encodes edit count. Some cases concentrated nearly all effort in a single phase; others spread work across all four. The British Politics BLP case is the clearest example of balance.

18 / Day × Hour Heatmaps

The Exact Moment of Political Debate

Each cell is one hour of one weekday. Brighter = more edits. These 9 small multiples reveal each case's signature "fighting schedule" — when editors were most active, and how that pattern differs across cultures and eras.

19 / Recurring Editor Network

The Persistent Few

Editors who participated in more than one politics arbitration case. Case nodes are arranged in a circle; editor nodes float between the cases they appeared in. Some editors are central to the entire political conflict ecosystem on Wikipedia.

20 / Phase Race

Which Phase Led at Each Moment?

A "race" chart: at each point in normalized case time, which phase had accumulated the most edits? The vertical stacking shows how evidence, workshop, and proposed decision jockeyed for dominance. Hover to explore individual cases.

Data from Wikipedia Arbitration Requests  ·  SARC 5400  ·  UVA
