"I do not paint things, I paint only the differences between things." -- Henri Matisse, 1943

Use spatial structure and visual thinking to explore, organize, and bring out relationships within and between data.  Extract something interesting, useful, or meaningful through that structure.

Your task is to explore, organize, and extract – to paint a picture – with data, through visualization.

What can you `draw` out of this?  Explore the difference between `charting/plotting` and `visualizing`.

Produce a single, fully developed, static visualization as product of the above.  Use the conceptual approaches, techniques, and thought processes that we have been studying, along with tools like Tableau, RAWGraphs, and Illustrator/Inkscape, to do this.

You do NOT need to code for this assignment.  
If you do code, do not get caught in it.  Visualize, don't summarize.


This is real data, and large, exactly as industry has.

How can we use graphical / spatial structure, with the precision of data, to see order and to construct comparison?  What can we discover about music, the act of listening, or the people doing so?   Can we expose relationships between things through purposeful arrangements of visual structure?

    What is your question?  What do you want to know?  What measures, orders, benchmarks, intersections, or contradictions might you organize around?
    What is your visual / organizational strategy?  How can graphical order construct relationships, and highlight them?  Can we draw those relationships to the screen instead of just drawing data points?  This is not something that you do AFTER looking at the data, but rather IN ORDER TO look at the data.  This *is* Exploratory Data Analysis.  Learn from our Visual Data Analysis exercise.
    Just Try!  Don't be afraid.  We don't expect you to resolve some major world problem here, but rather that you position – through visual/spatial construction – a strategy to try to understand and expose an idea.  Sketch with data.  Build targeted graphical tests.  Show and talk about your attempts to see into this.
    Use as much or as little of the data as you want.  This could be about one artist or one track, or about the entire set on a particular pattern.  Filter it down to something that you're curious to see.  Sort, join, extract, or otherwise wrangle the data further if you like, and if you need help doing this to focus or manage the data, just let me know.  Keep it targeted and don't try to do everything.  Do something purposefully.
    Use visual / spatial structure as an analytic strategy.  There is a difference between plotting and visualizing.  We're doing the latter.  Please don't just run some posterizing statistical analysis and dump that reduction to the screen.  Consider density and range as richness and texture.  Use spatial quality to expose detail, scale, distance from a benchmark, outlier, cluster.  Use highlighting, opacity, etc. to reveal a part within a whole or a pattern that's interesting.
    Extract.  So what?  Right there on the visual.  From your structure.  That's what the structure is for.  
    Don't let the tool drive the process.  Use any of the tools that we have been learning, but this is not about the tools.  Be intentional.
    Plotting is a graphic dump.  Visualization is analysis.
