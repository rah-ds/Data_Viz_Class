What Drives Wikipedia to Its Court of Last Resort?

A visual analysis of 481 Wikipedia Arbitration Committee cases — examining the categories, dispute types, and escalation pathways that lead to binding arbitration on the world's largest encyclopedia.

Visualization 1: The Escalation Funnel

A Sankey diagram mapping dispute flow through Wikipedia's graduated resolution system

Flow width encodes the volume of disputes moving between stages. The leftward funnel of Talk Page → Resolved shows that most disputes never escalate — but those that do travel increasingly narrow, higher-authority channels. Conduct disputes (warm colors) skip content-focused stages and go directly to admin intervention.

Visualization 2: The Anatomy of Arbitration

A nested treemap showing case categories subdivided by dispute type — area encodes case count

Area maps directly to the number of cases. The overwhelming dominance of the "Editor Disputes" rectangle reveals that ArbCom functions primarily as a behavioral court — most cases bear an individual editor's name rather than a topic. Within topic-driven categories, geopolitics and social/cultural issues dominate.

Visualization 3: Which Disputes Come Back?

A heatmap of category × complexity, with color saturation encoding the proportion of recurring cases

Color intensity encodes the proportion of recurring cases (cases with numbered sequels like "Arbitration enforcement 2"). Darker cells indicate dispute categories and complexity levels that resist resolution even after a prior ArbCom ruling. Cell annotations show count / recurrence rate.
