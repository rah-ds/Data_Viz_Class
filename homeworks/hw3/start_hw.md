Visual Data Analysis
Snake Oil Supplements, Information is Beautiful

Having surveyed information graphics (assignment 1) and then built one on your own (assignment 2) we will now start to more critically analyze data visualization for specific approach, technique, and effectiveness at analyzing a problem or question. We will also begin to use some existing visualization tools, and ask how they can be useful and what their limitations are.

For this assignment, choose a single dataset of your liking and produce three (3) prototype visualizations of it or some component of it, each using a different technique, and then critically compare your techniques and their results for effectiveness at seeing into the data and contributing insight.

There are two foci for this assignment:

    To explore and test out different approaches and techniques toward visual structure, differentiation, encoding, and analysis that are out there amidst available tools.  There are some novel approaches here.  Try them.
    To use these techniques to critically analyze graphical, structural, organizational, and representational approaches for their strengths and weaknesses at gaining insight into information.

All three of your visuals should be built upon the same dataset, and each should take a very different visual or organizational approach.  Use the same or similar data fields across the three visuals for better comparison, though some visual approaches may work better with some variable types over others, and some strategies will allow for more (or constrain to fewer) variables to show.  Allow for this, use it, and critique it. 

The data chosen can be nearly anything, and can be from a current project elsewhere, your capstone, or just from one of the many sample data sites on the internet (see Resource Links).  Ideally this will be data that does not require any wrangling.

Your three visuals do not need to be fully polished, final-presentation work, but each should be complete enough to show something useful and possibly insightful about the data and the questions that you are asking.  The purpose in this assignment is to compare techniques on precisely how they each do this.  

Also begin to get into the habit to export or extract (SVG, PDF, copy/paste) from the data tool, and then arrange, annotate, and clean up in Inkscape or Illustrator.  This can help to add clarity, meaning, and organization to what the tool produced.  All tools are only a starting point and a lens into the data.  You are the author!

Consider, and in your writeup specifically respond to, the following:

    What is your question?
        What specific problem or question are you asking of this dataset?  What are you trying to know or resolve?
        Consider making your title a version of that question.

    What visualization approaches and techniques are you using, and how do they work?
        Visually and structurally, how are you organizing and encoding the data to see into it?  What specific techniques are you trying, and why?  How are you using color, shape, weight, location, ...?  Consider the strategies that we have been discussing and naming.  Use our discussions of cognitive load theory, chunking, schema, split attention, .... Refer back to Tufte's `Escaping Flatland` and `Grand Principles`.
        How does your chosen visual strategy construct "rules" for where and how data elements and relationships appear?

    What insight did you gain?
        Specifically, do you discover something about your question?  What?
        Why? How does your visual / organizational technique help (or hurt) to do so?

    Compare your techniques critically and suggest improvements.
        Does one technique or approach show something different or better than another?  What specifically does each reveal that the others do not? 
        What does each one hide or fail to show?
        Compare how the rules of layout affect your ability to see.  How useful are these rules at organizing, comparing, exposing, and increasing your understanding of the issue(s)?  
        What could be done to improve your understanding of this data and question?  What might be next steps?
        Critique your strategies!

Content is important, but the spatial/graphical/cognitive techniques used are more important. Pick your techniques apart, using the prompts above, and critique how design specifically furthers content. This is not about how to nicely display data, or coming up with a cool visualization, but rather an analysis of how graphic strategies help (or hinder) us to see.

These tools are built to be quick, so experiment, explore, and iterate.

What to turn in:

    Your set of visualizations, as a PDF or set of PDFs. You can combine into one document, with analysis, or split out separately.
    Written analysis on your selected tools, techniques, and critiques, responding to the questions above.

What I'm looking for:
A direct comparison of the strengths and weaknesses of three very different visual techniques / organizing principles, to explore and understand the same data set.

As usual, we will review your analyses collectively in class, and through peer review.
